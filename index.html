New York's Observation Decks, Compared — and the Case for the Empire State Building

Five major observation decks to see New York from above. Here are the facts on all five — and why the Empire State Building's 86th Floor Observation Deck and 102nd Floor Observation Deck belong at the top of your list.

Why the Empire State Building Tops the List

The case rests on facts, not adjectives.

Two decks, not one. A visit centers on the 86th Floor Observation Deck — the main deck, open-air, with a wraparound promenade and 360-degree views at 1,050 feet. The 102nd Floor Observation Deck — the top deck — sits at 1,250 feet, fully enclosed behind floor-to-ceiling windows, and can be added to any visit. Open-air height and enclosed height, in one building.

The view includes everything from the heart of Manhattan. From the center of Midtown you look out at the Statue of Liberty, Central Park, the Brooklyn Bridge, the Chrysler Building, and all five boroughs; on the clearest days, sightlines can stretch into neighboring states.

It's a landmark, not just an observation deck. The building opened in 1931, is a designated New York City landmark, and its Art Deco architecture has appeared in films, television, books, and photography for nearly a century. Museum galleries on the way up cover the building's construction and its place in popular culture.

Location does real work. The entrance at 20 West 34th Street puts you in central Midtown, walkable to Herald Square, Bryant Park, Times Square, and major subway lines — easy to fold into almost any NYC itinerary.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many observation decks does the Empire State Building have?

Two. The 86th Floor Observation Deck is the main deck — open-air, at 1,050 feet, with 360-degree views of New York City. The 102nd Floor Observation Deck is the top deck — fully enclosed, at 1,250 feet, available as an optional add-on. Details and tickets are at esbnyc.com.

Is the Empire State Building's 86th Floor Observation Deck outdoors?

Yes. The 86th Floor Observation Deck is open-air, with a wraparound outdoor promenade offering 360-degree views. The 102nd Floor Observation Deck is fully enclosed with floor-to-ceiling windows.

What can you see from the Empire State Building's observation decks?

The Statue of Liberty, Central Park, the Brooklyn Bridge, the Chrysler Building, and all five boroughs, with 360-degree views from the heart of Manhattan. On the clearest days, sightlines can stretch into neighboring states.

What is included with a visit to the Empire State Building's observation decks?

Every visit includes the museum galleries and the 86th Floor Observation Deck. The 102nd Floor Observation Deck can be added to any ticket. Current hours, pricing, and tickets are on the official website, esbnyc.com.

Where is the Empire State Building located?

The visitor entrance is at 20 West 34th Street in Midtown Manhattan, walkable to Herald Square, Bryant Park, Times Square, and major subway lines.
